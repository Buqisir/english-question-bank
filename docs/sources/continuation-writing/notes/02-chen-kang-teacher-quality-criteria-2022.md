# S02 — 陈康：教师视角下读后续写的质量评价标准探析

Source: 陈康，教育部教育考试院，《中小学外语教学（中学篇）》
2022 年第 3 期，第 61–65 页.

## Source role

This is high-weight examination-authority research, but it is not itself the
official scoring rubric. Its special value is that it compares teachers'
internalized criteria with the official rubric and explicitly identifies both
accurate elaborations and distortions.

## Study design

- 408 questionnaires were returned; 391 were valid.
- Participants came from 46 high schools in seven cities and represented all
  three high-school grades and varied teaching experience (pp. 61–62).
- Teachers independently described high- and low-level continuations without
  being prompted to copy the official rubric. Two experts inductively coded
  the descriptions; 12 teachers then participated in follow-up interviews
  (pp. 61–62).

This method means the detailed categories describe **teacher attention and
operationalization**, not automatically official subcriteria.

## Four observed dimensions

The coding framework contains content quality, language quality, coherence,
and writing conventions (pp. 61–63). Content and language account for nearly
80% of all description units:

| Response level | Content | Language | Coherence | Conventions | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| High | 489 | 487 | 226 | 94 | 1296 |
| Low | 470 | 438 | 137 | 82 | 1127 |

### Content quality

The teacher-derived content categories are source understanding, source
connection, theme, logic, richness, and completeness. Logic receives the most
attention, followed by source connection. Low-level responses receive much
more comment on source misunderstanding (pp. 62–63).

“Theme” is a notable interpretation rather than a literal rubric item. Teachers
use it for a central idea inferred from the source and for a positive value
orientation. Chen treats this as a possible deeper reading of situation fit and
as an expression of the educational mission; it should not be flattened into a
mandatory explicit moral sentence (pp. 63–64).

### Language quality

The teacher-derived categories are accuracy, diversity, appropriateness,
complexity, and descriptive language. Accuracy dominates comments on weak
responses; descriptive language is common in comments on strong responses
(pp. 62–63).

Crucially, “descriptive language” is not a direct item in the official rubric.
Its prevalence is associated with the historical use of narrative source texts.
Whether description should be used, and what kind, must be determined by the
source's content and language (pp. 63–64).

### Coherence and conventions

Teachers use broad descriptions such as smooth/coherent for the former, and
word count, legibility, and similar surface requirements for the latter. Chen
warns that undefined labels such as “beautiful and fluent” or “highly readable”
cannot give effective feedback (pp. 63–65).

## The most important cautions

1. Mechanical requirements for environment, psychology, action, or other
   description narrow the construct. Requiring memorized descriptive chunks is
   test coaching, not source-responsive continuation (p. 64).
2. A single global label hides different learner profiles. One strong response
   may be imaginative but error-prone; another linguistically accurate but
   thin in content. Analysis must preserve the distinction (p. 64).
3. Teaching descriptors should be concrete, observable, accurately defined,
   and understood by both teacher and learner (pp. 64–65).
4. The core remains read–write alignment and learning promotion: understand,
   see through, write, and continue well. Technique must not replace this
   construct (pp. 64–65).

## Later field-design pressure (not a field decision)

- Preserve separable evidence for content, language, coherence, and mechanical
  conventions even if a holistic judgment is later rendered.
- Capture source understanding separately from invented-content quality.
- Make any genre-specific descriptive feature conditional, never universal.
- Prefer evidence-bearing descriptors over adjectives such as “excellent” or
  “vivid”.
- Support diagnostic asymmetry: strong content/weak language and weak
  content/strong language are materially different analyses.

## Limits

The data report teachers' language about student work, not direct coding of the
student scripts. The province is anonymized. Frequency does not establish
scoring weight, and the paper does not define a machine-readable schema.
