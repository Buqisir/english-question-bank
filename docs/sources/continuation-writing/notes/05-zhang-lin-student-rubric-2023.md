## Source role and limitation

This is a classroom-evaluation proposal centered on making scoring criteria
visible to students and involving them in self- and peer-assessment. It is a
low-authority interpretation for field semantics. Although published in 2023,
its student rubric reproduces the 2015 keyword-era requirement, so “latest” in
the research narrative does not make its rubric current.

The paper reports a school mock examination, rater training, and a many-facet
Rasch analysis. Several method and formula descriptions are too compressed or
unclear in the supplied pages to support a reliable statistical inference. The
analysis fields should not be derived from those numbers.

## Useful contribution

The strongest contribution is about **use of evaluation**, not about adding
new scoring dimensions:

- students should understand band descriptors rather than see only a score;
- self-assessment and peer assessment can make revision criteria actionable;
- a response should receive multidimensional feedback on organization,
  content/language, logic, and coherence;
- middle-band exemplars and rater discussion can improve consistency;
- evaluation should support subsequent revision and reader awareness.

This suggests that a future analysis should be readable and traceable enough
for a teacher or learner to compare the response with evidence and revise it.
It does **not** suggest that student judgment replaces official scoring.

## What must not be imported as current doctrine

- marked-keyword counts;
- the old five-band wording presented as a current rubric;
- any apparent one-to-one conversion from analytic indicators to an exact
  holistic score;
- statistical conclusions that cannot be reconstructed from the supplied
  description;
- the assumption that repeated testing alone validates the semantic categories.

## Later field-design pressure (not a field decision)

Keep machine analysis capable of producing a concise, evidence-bearing feedback
view and revision targets, but version scoring policy separately from analysis
evidence. A band explanation should show why the response belongs there and
what the nearest improvement is, rather than expose a pseudo-precise sum of
checkboxes.
