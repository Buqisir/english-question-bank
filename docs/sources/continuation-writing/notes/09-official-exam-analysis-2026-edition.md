# S09 — Official `高考试题分析（2026版）·英语`

Source: 教育部教育考试院编，语文出版社，2025. The supplied PDF is
the official analysis of the two 2025 national papers.

The entire 115-page PDF was OCR-indexed. Relevant evidence was visually
checked on PDF pp. 6–9 (printed pp. 1–4, overall evaluation) and 108–115
(printed pp. 105–112, both continuation tasks and analyses).

## Authority and artifact boundary

This is the newest supplied official task analysis. It contains task text,
construct commentary, and possible response developments, but not English
model continuations for the two tasks. The possibilities are authoritative
examples of valid reasoning, not exhaustive answer keys.

## Overall official positioning

The evaluation calls both continuation tasks open designs that provide space
for divergent and innovative thought. One requires an apology that repairs a
brotherly relationship; the other asks an international student to explain a
Chinese name and turn discomfort into intercultural sharing (PDF pp. 7–9).

This pairing matters: the second task is not simply a conventional action-
conflict-resolution story. It mixes personal narrative, explanation, classroom
interaction, cultural meaning, and communicative purpose.

## Shared construct and current quality criteria

For both tasks, the official analysis asks the writer to understand the source,
reason about subsequent development, create reasonable content, and express it
accurately and coherently with appropriate vocabulary and grammar. The task is
an integrated reading/writing assessment with room for imagination (PDF pp.
109 and 113–114).

For both tasks, quality is evaluated from:

1. created-content quality, continuation completeness, and fit with the source
   situation;
2. vocabulary/grammar accuracy, appropriateness, and diversity;
3. contextual cohesion and whole-text coherence (PDF pp. 112 and 115).

This is official confirmation of the three-part current rubric seen in S01 and
S08.

## Task A — brother, dog, and apology

The source supplies a character/situation model: the narrator excluded the
brother's dog from the house; the brother is unwell, recently bereaved, and
relies on the dog; the narrator initially resists apologizing; the narrator's
wife is considerate and has already encouraged contact. Starter one marks the
recognition of fault. Starter two fixes a later state: the narrator arrives at
the brother's door with biscuits made by the wife.

The official analysis permits different paragraph-one paths: reflection on
better ways to accommodate the dog, discussion with the wife, deciding on an
apology, and preparation of the biscuits. Paragraph two needs a plausible
doorstep/apology/reconciliation development and closure. The optional details
are alternatives; the fixed dependencies are recognition → repair plan →
arrival → apology/reconciliation (PDF pp. 110–112).

The analysis infers traits from distributed source evidence: the wife is
considerate and capable; the narrator has been self-centered but can reflect
and repair. Character inference is therefore evidence-based and revisable, not
a free label.

## Task B — the meaning of a Chinese name

The source establishes pronunciation difficulty, embarrassment, avoidance,
and recognition that avoiding correction sacrifices an opportunity to share
cultural identity. Starter one places the narrator in a class discussion
explaining the name's meaning. Starter two fixes classmates' interest and
post-class approach.

The first paragraph must perform an explanatory/communicative job: make the
name's meaning intelligible. The official analysis permits optional supporting
material such as why the parents chose it, a seasonal/holiday association, or
classroom interaction. The second paragraph develops or elevates the exchange:
Chinese naming culture and parental hopes, helping classmates choose Chinese
names, or continuing cultural communication are examples (PDF pp. 114–115).

The task's logic is not a narrative climax template. Its state transition is
embarrassment/withdrawal → opportunity to explain → successful audience
interest → further intercultural communication. Audience response and achieved
communicative effect are central.

## Strongest combined inference from the two tasks

Current official continuation analysis needs a shared core broad enough for:

- event/relationship repair in a narrative;
- explanation, audience design, and intercultural communication in a mixed
  personal/expository text.

Plot, emotional arc, action detail, and dialogue may be useful in Task A but
cannot be universal. Purpose, audience, discourse move, and boundary-state
logic are the more stable shared concepts.

## Cautions

- Optional details introduced with “can” are not hidden scoring keys.
- Official value framing should not be converted into compulsory political or
  moral phrasing in a student response.
- “Vivid/story-like” wording in the name-task analysis describes one possible
  realization; explanatory clarity and communicative success remain essential.
- The book supplies no English model response here, so none should be invented
  and attributed to it.
