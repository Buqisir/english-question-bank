# S11 — `普通高中英语课程标准（2017年版2020年修订）`

This older official standard is less current than S10, but it supplies direct
evidence that continuation was explicitly designed as an integrated reading-
writing activity and an examination form.

## Explicit continuation references

### Integrated-skills teaching

Reading work can include prediction, extracting information, discussion,
summary writing, and continuation, combining reading/viewing/speaking/writing
instead of training isolated skills (PDF p. 48, printed p. 40).

### Academic quality level 2

Level 2—the stated main basis for Gaokao—includes the ability to:

- identify intent, important information, fact/view relations, cultural
  background, and implicit meaning;
- understand new/old information flow and semantic relations such as sequence,
  cause, generalization/example, and temporal/spatial/process order;
- understand word function, intention, and the role of language in attitude and
  value;
- describe experience, state views and emotion, describe event development,
  describe people/things, explain concepts, summarize what was read, **or
  continue a text**;
- deliberately select vocabulary and grammar to express meaning accurately and
  with logical relations (PDF pp. 56–57, printed pp. 48–49).

The list itself already extends beyond narrative: description, explanation,
viewpoint, and conceptual meaning sit beside continuation.

### Examination form

The exam-design section names continuation among a diverse set of forms and
asks for broad, scientific, fair coverage (PDF p. 101, printed p. 93). In its
illustrative writing forms, it gives “story continuation”: read a narrative and
continue its plot to make the story complete. Writing is introduced as
assessing fluency, accuracy, and appropriateness (PDF pp. 114–115, printed
pp. 106–107).

This is explicit but illustrative. It says story continuation is one possible
form, not that all future continuation tasks must be stories.

## Supporting language-skill requirements

The standard defines receptive and productive skills as mutually supporting.
For selective mandatory study it requires writers to:

- describe/summary experience and fact;
- convey information, argue views, and express emotion;
- clarify through repetition, examples, and explanation;
- use cohesion for coherence;
- choose language for intention and audience;
- design a reasonable discourse structure (PDF pp. 43–47, printed
  pp. 35–39).

Learning strategies include identifying source type/purpose, understanding
logic and paragraph cohesion, organizing information by theme, using cohesion,
and planning/drafting/revising/editing (PDF pp. 49–51, printed pp. 41–43).

## Stable value when combined with the new revision

S11 supplies the missing explicit link: continuation is a legitimate integrated
language task and may directly assess continuing a read text. S10 supplies the
newer and wider specification of what high-school writing and discourse ability
mean today.

Accordingly:

- retain the explicit `read → understand relations → continue` task identity;
- do not retain “story” as a universal type restriction;
- interpret completeness by the discourse purpose and supplied starters, not
  solely by plot resolution;
- use the current level-2 language/pragmatic/discourse requirements from S10
  when old and new wording differ.

## Cautions

- The old standard does not define the current five-band rubric.
- Its illustrative story-continuation description is not evidence for mandatory
  Freytag structure, emotional curves, dialogue, or descriptive detail.
- Level 3 and optional-course requirements remain outside the Gaokao baseline.
- The old standard's marked task categories cannot override current official
  tasks showing mixed/expository continuation.
