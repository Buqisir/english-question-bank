# S01 — Current rubric screenshot (non-official)

## Provenance boundary

The owner explicitly identifies this screenshot as non-official while noting
that its wording corresponds to the newest continuation-writing task. It is
therefore useful for detecting a rubric change, but it cannot independently
establish the issuing body, year, or exact task. Any future production rule
must be confirmed against an official task or official annual analysis.

## What the screenshot says

The screenshot presents a 25-point, five-band, holistic rubric. The initial
band is chosen from the response as a whole, then adjusted and scored within
that band. It asks the rater to consider three connected areas:

1. quality and completeness of the continuation and its fit with the original
   situation;
2. accuracy, appropriateness, and diversity of vocabulary and grammatical
   structures;
3. local cohesion and whole-text coherence.

The fifth-band descriptors operationalize the first area more concretely:
close relation to the source, reasonable connection to both paragraph starters,
novel and rich yet reasonable/logical content, and a complete continuation.
The language and coherence descriptions descend across the lower bands. A zero
response is either not meaningful, copied from the source, or unrelated to the
task.

The supplementary rules shown are about word count, legibility, spelling, and
punctuation. British and American spelling/usage are both acceptable. These
belong to response conventions and penalty handling; they should not be mixed
into the semantic analysis of the continuation.

## Change signal

Compared with the older keyword-era rubric reproduced in S03/S04, this wording:

- no longer rewards coverage of marked keywords;
- names content **quality and completeness** directly;
- makes source-situation fit explicit;
- adds **appropriateness** and **diversity** alongside language accuracy;
- retains both sentence-to-sentence connection and whole-text coherence.

This is a meaningful construct signal, not merely editorial wording. A future
analysis schema must not carry the obsolete `keywordCoverage` requirement
forward.

## What this source does not justify

- It does not prove that every task is narrative.
- It does not prescribe action, psychology, dialogue, environmental detail,
  emotional arcs, a moral ending, or “show, don't tell”.
- It does not give fixed weights for content, language, and coherence.
- It does not make one possible plot or official model answer mandatory.
- Its lack of provenance prevents it from serving as the sole production
  authority.

## Later field-design pressure (not a field decision)

The wording demands evidence capable of explaining whole-response banding
without reducing it to independent checkboxes. At minimum, later work must be
able to retain evidence for source fit, starter fit, completeness, logical
development, language quality, and coherence while keeping mechanical penalty
rules separate.
