# S04 — 陈康：高考英语启用读后续写题型的效果验证研究

Source: 陈康，教育部考试中心，原载《中小学外语教学（中学篇）》
2019 年第 11 期，第 1–6 页；the supplied `.doc` is an HTML archive of a
2020 reprint. The three linked table images were also recovered and inspected.

## Source role

This is a high-weight task-designer validation study. It explains the intended
construct, tests teachers' use of the scoring standard against expert-scored
responses, and states where common teaching practice does or does not match the
designers' expectations.

## Intended construct

The task combines reading and writing to assess:

- understanding and analysis of the source;
- reasonable creation of new content on that basis;
- appropriate and accurate written expression;
- construction of a complete, coherent continuation.

Table 1 expands this into reading, writing, thinking, and learning abilities.
Reading includes source content, discourse structure, key information, logic,
language features, and other content elements. Writing includes new content,
content fit, rich/accurate language, higher-level alignment with source
vocabulary/sentence patterns/style, connection to the source, and effective
cohesive means. Thinking appears as source-constrained creativity; learning as
learning the source author's language and writing methods.

This table is the clearest supplied designer-side explanation of what older
rubric terms such as “fit” and “reasonable connection” are trying to elicit.

## Study design and results

- More than 600 students at two Zhejiang high schools completed a full mock
  paper containing a continuation task.
- Two experienced experts scored the responses; disagreements over five points
  were resolved by discussion.
- Twenty typical scripts were selected: five in band 5, six in band 4, five in
  band 3, and two each in bands 2 and 1.
- Twelve teachers from five schools independently scored the typical scripts
  among randomized distractor scripts, then completed retrospective interviews.

Most teacher ratings correlated strongly with expert ratings. Ten of twelve
teachers had no more than two scripts differing by at least one five-point band;
two teachers were clear outliers. Teacher scoring was generally stricter and
showed regression toward the middle, with more disagreement on middle-band
scripts than at the extremes (Tables 2–3).

This warns against treating a detailed analytic output as an automatic exact
score: the official procedure is holistic band placement, and middle levels
contain genuine judgment uncertainty.

## Interview evidence

Teachers describe three main ability families:

### Reading

Deep and accurate understanding includes theme, character, plot, logical
structure, callbacks/context relations, and the author's style and rhetoric.
The continuation reveals how well this deep processing occurred.

### Writing

The writer must use rich vocabulary and grammatical resources accurately and
appropriately for the whole situation, with natural and compact connection.

### Thinking

Imagination is required but remains constrained by logic, theme, source and
starter connection, and alignment with the author's thought, method, and style.

Teachers disagreed over whether content/thinking or language was primary. The
designer-side conclusion is that content and language are equally important:
without content and language alignment, the intended read–write interaction
and learning effect is weakened.

## What “alignment” means here

The paper explains source/starter fit as interaction with:

- strong language,
- ideas,
- rigorous logic,
- effective cohesion and coherence.

The process is: understand the source, construct a situation model, create new
content within that model, and make both content and language cohere with the
source. This is materially richer than keyword reuse or surface lexical
matching.

## Explicit warnings

- The marked-keyword rule produced inconsistent interpretations and could
  invite forced insertion. It was part of an older task version, not the core
  construct.
- “Stringing words into a text” cannot replace source understanding and a
  situation model.
- Teacher agreement at the broad level does not eliminate ambiguity in
  “high fit”, “reasonable connection”, or content/language balance.
- The official rubric is intentionally compact; operational detail belongs in
  careful scoring guidance and training, not arbitrary classroom invention.

## Later field-design pressure (not a field decision)

- Treat source comprehension, constrained creation, expression, and coherence
  as related but distinguishable evidence families.
- Represent alignment as evidence-backed relations, not a similarity score.
- Preserve source language/style evidence without demanding imitation of every
  visible feature.
- Record uncertainty and holistic-band reasoning, especially for middle-level
  responses.
- Exclude obsolete keyword-count requirements and template-like “content
  richness” proxies.
