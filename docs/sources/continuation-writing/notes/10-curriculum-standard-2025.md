# S10 — `普通高中英语课程标准（2017年版2025年修订）`

This is the current official curriculum authority in the supplied set. It does
not name continuation writing, but its updated writing, discourse, pragmatic,
thinking, quality, task-design, and scoring requirements govern how the task
should be interpreted now.

## Governing level for Gaokao

The standard states that Gaokao requirements do not exceed academic quality
level 2 (PDF p. 51, printed p. 47). Level 3 may guide optional advanced courses
or other assessments, but cannot be silently treated as a Gaokao scoring
baseline.

This blocks a common inflation error: “naturally and appropriately using
complex sentences and diverse cohesive means” belongs to level 3, not level 2
(PDF p. 50, printed p. 46). Current continuation analysis may recognize such
strengths, but must not require them for basic task success or convert them into
a rigid complexity quota.

## Current construct at level 2

### Understanding the source

The learner identifies relationships among knowledge from a text's type,
structure, and language; explains logical relations; understands the intent of
specific language and its relation to attitude/value; and interprets meaning,
speaker/writer intention, and attitude in context (PDF p. 48, printed p. 44).

This supports a source model that includes discourse purpose/structure,
information and relations, implicit intention, attitude, and context—not only
narrative characters and plot.

### Producing the continuation

At level 2 the learner can describe, explain, interpret, argue, and evaluate,
with expression that is clear, appropriate, and coherent; can analyze differing
views and make evidence-based claims (PDF p. 48, printed p. 44). The language-
skills table adds:

- choose a reasonable discourse structure and organize content logically for
  the intended purpose;
- choose appropriate sentences and words for purpose and audience;
- use cohesive resources to achieve coherence (PDF p. 37, printed p. 34).

These are broad enough for narrative, expository, argumentative, and mixed
continuations.

### Communication and culture

The standard treats audience, role, formality, directness/indirectness,
politeness, emotional distance, and intercultural context as part of language
use. The aim is effective, appropriate communication—not only grammatical
correctness (PDF pp. 29–30, printed pp. 25–26; PDF pp. 47–49, printed
pp. 43–45).

This is directly relevant to the 2025 name task and any continuation whose
success depends on apology, persuasion, explanation, or relationship repair.

## Discourse-type requirements

The standard includes narrative, expository, argumentative, application,
news, new-media, and other discourse types across mandatory and selective
courses (PDF pp. 17–18, printed pp. 14–15).

Discourse knowledge covers communicative purpose, structure, language
features, explicit cohesion, starter/topic/transition sentences, new/old
information flow, and semantic relations such as sequence, cause, and
generalization/example. It explicitly says type structures and features are not
fixed and must be analyzed in the concrete text (PDF pp. 25–28, printed
pp. 22–24).

This confirms S07's warning: genre supplies expectations, not a hard template.

## Language quality is contextual

- Vocabulary is selected in context to name, refer, describe processes and
  features, explain concepts, and accurately express meaning (PDF pp. 20–21,
  printed pp. 17–19).
- Grammar is a unity of form, meaning, and use; selection depends on context and
  pragmatic intent (PDF pp. 23–25, printed pp. 20–22).
- Discourse organization includes micro relations inside/among sentences and
  macro relations among paragraphs, whole-text purpose, and theme (PDF pp.
  25–27, printed pp. 22–24).
- Pragmatic quality depends on purpose, participant, role, occasion, and
  cultural context (PDF pp. 29–30, printed pp. 25–26).

Therefore accuracy, appropriateness, diversity, and coherence need functional
evidence. Raw counts of advanced vocabulary, clauses, or connectors are not the
construct.

## Reading-to-writing process

The current standard asks the reader to identify discourse type, structure,
language features, events, key information, logical relations, and author
strategy, then create and revise a text for a purpose. Writing includes
planning, drafting, revision, and proofreading (PDF pp. 34–39, printed
pp. 31–35). Teaching guidance asks for source study of theme, content, purpose,
genre, structure, language, and viewpoint, while acknowledging that some
interpretation is not unique (PDF pp. 52–53, printed pp. 48–49).

This supports analysis that explains evidence and alternatives rather than
pretends there is a single invisible plot.

## Exam and scoring guidance

Gaokao should use both comprehension and productive skills, include fictional
and non-fictional material, create authentic/open language-use and problem-
solving contexts, and design tasks that elicit the target construct (PDF
pp. 64–67, printed pp. 57–60).

Written-expression scoring should consider:

- how fully/intelligibly content communicates meaning;
- lexical/grammatical accuracy and appropriateness;
- discourse fluency (PDF p. 68, printed p. 61).

This is general scoring guidance, not a replacement for the current official
continuation rubric in S08/S09.

## What the absence of “continuation” means—and does not mean

The revision no longer lists continuation as a named teaching activity or
story-continuation test form. It instead uses broader categories of integrated
skills, different written genres, authentic/open tasks, and quality-based
scoring. This does not abolish the task: the newer official 2026 analysis in
S09 still uses it. The defensible reading is that continuation must now be
located inside the broader current construct, not frozen to the 2020 example of
story completion.

## Later field-design pressure (not a field decision)

- Tag evidence with curriculum level; do not apply level-3 expectations as
  level-2 failures.
- Make purpose, audience, discourse type/structure, pragmatic fit, and
  communicative effect part of the shared analysis vocabulary.
- Condition language observations on form–meaning–use and discourse function.
- Preserve open alternative interpretations when the source supports them.
