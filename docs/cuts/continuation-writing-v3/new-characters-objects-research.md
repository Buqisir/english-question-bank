# New characters and new objects in continuations (2026-11-24)

Question from teachers' practice: may a continuation bring in new characters or
new objects? The common view is that new characters are risky and new objects
are unclear. This note records what the sources and essays show; the decision
is the Owner's.

## Sources checked

- Expert and official notes in `docs/sources/continuation-writing/notes/`.
  None states a rule on new characters or objects. The official analyses
  (notes 08, 09) do list optional developments that add things: a means of
  contact the narrator must have for the later phone call, classmates asking
  for Chinese names. The rubric asks for content that is new and rich yet
  reasonable, and that fits the source situation.
- Ten reference essays for three stories (2024 national, 2025 national I and
  II): four teacher essays, five student essays, one exam essay. Only counts
  and categories are kept here, no text.
- Round-2 trial outputs: 7 V3 papers (standard and advanced) and 4 V2 papers.

Method: nouns and proper nouns in each essay whose lemma is absent from the
source and starters (spaCy `en_core_web_sm`), then each person word read in its
sentence and sorted by hand.

## What the reference essays do

New people appear in two ways only:

- people the source or starters already imply, made concrete but left unnamed
  and in a reacting role: classmates in the name story ("a girl", "a
  classmate");
- off-stage family mentioned in a clause, not acting in the scene: parents who
  chose the name, the narrator's son and granddaughter.

No reference essay names a new character, and none lets a new person drive the
turn or solve the problem.

New objects appear in all ten essays: a pen and a written number, a name card,
a gift, a banknote, a bag, flowers, a tin of biscuits, a plate, tea, a poem.
Each is something the characters would plausibly have at hand, and each does a
job in the action (giving a means of contact, repaying, apologising, showing
welcome). None appears from nowhere to solve the central problem.

## What our trial outputs do

- Most new people are implied by the source or starters: children at the
  reading-corner opening, passengers in the bus queue, a judge at the award
  presentation, teammates at the finish. Family mentions are grounded in the
  source (the narrator's family from Brazil).
- Two cases go further than any reference essay:
  - 2025 national II: both V3 essays name a new classmate, "Emma". The
    classmates are implied by starter two; the name is not.
  - 2024.1 Zhejiang: all three essays (V3 standard, V3 advanced, V2) end with a
    new student asking Eva the way, so that Eva's new skill is proved through a
    stranger. The school is full of students, so the person is plausible, but
    a new person carries the closing scene.
- New objects follow the reference pattern: notebooks, a box of chocolates, a
  receipt, a towel, a certificate, a poster. All are props within reach.

## Reading

- The teachers' view on new characters holds in a narrower form: what the
  references avoid is a new person who matters to the plot, and naming one.
  People the situation already implies may appear in the background or as a
  reaction.
- New objects are normal. The line is the one principle 4 already draws
  against help from the sky: an object may serve the action, but must not
  solve the problem by luck.
- Small samples: ten reference essays over three stories, eleven trial essays.
  No official text settles the question.
